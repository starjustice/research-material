1 · The six design steps

Say them out loud at the start of any "design the tables" question. They buy you thinking time and show a method.

2 · Normalization: one bad table becomes four good ones

Each fact lives in one place. The foreign key always sits on the "many" side.

Say this: "Every non-key column depends on the key, the whole key, and nothing but the key. And unit_price on the order line is not duplication: it is the price at order time, a different fact from today's price."

3 · Ledger: never just overwrite a balance

Every change is a new row with a reason and a source. The balance is the sum. A unique source key makes duplicates impossible.

Say this: "For anything that moves value I keep an append-only ledger. The balance is a cache of the sum, so I can always explain and rebuild it, and the unique source key means a retried event can never post twice."

4 · Why random UUIDs hurt a B-tree, and v7 does not

Say this: "Internal tables get bigint identity. IDs exposed to the outside get UUIDv7: not guessable, still time ordered so inserts stay at the right edge of the index. PostgreSQL 18 has uuidv7() built in."

Normal forms in one line each

FormRuleAsk yourself
1NFOne value per cell. No lists, no product1/product2 columns."Is this cell secretly a list?"
2NFWith a composite key, columns need the whole key."Does this column only need half the key?"
3NFNo column depends on another non-key column."If I know X, do I already know Y?"
BCNFStricter 3NF for overlapping keys. Rarely asked."3NF is the practical target."
