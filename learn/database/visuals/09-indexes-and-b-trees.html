The rule to remember: a search reads one index page per level, then only the table pages that hold the matches. The leaves are sorted and linked, so a range just walks to the right. Every INSERT must also write into every index, and a full leaf must be split in two.

  
Words on this page:
    
    
      
  • Page: a block of 8 kB on disk. Postgres reads and writes whole pages.
    • 
      
  • Root, inner page, leaf: the levels of the tree. The root is the top page. The leaves at the bottom hold every value with its row address.
    • 
      
  • Row address: page number and slot, like 3,2. Postgres calls it ctid. Pages are counted from 0.
    • 
      
  • Split: when a leaf is full, Postgres moves half of it to a new page and adds a pointer in the page above.
