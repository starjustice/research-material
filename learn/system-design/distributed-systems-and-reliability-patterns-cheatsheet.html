DISTRIBUTED SYSTEMS & RELIABILITY

Nines · Safe calls · Breakers and bulkheads · Health checks · Raft · Clocks and locks · Failure thinking

Every picture answers one question: what happens when a part dies or gets slow? Redraw each one from memory.

1 · The nines: parts in a row vs copies side by side

Say this: "Availability multiplies along the request path, so a long chain is fragile. Copies side by side, in different zones, remove the single points of failure."

2 · Retries multiply through layers, and the jitter formula

Say this: "Retries in every layer multiply load on the sick part. I retry in one layer, two or three times, with exponential backoff and full jitter, under a retry budget."

3 · Circuit breaker: closed, open, half open

Say this: "One breaker per dependency. When half the recent calls fail, it opens and we fail fast, so we stop waiting on timeouts and the sick service gets quiet time. After a cool down, a few test calls decide."

4 · The readiness trap: one slow database takes out every copy

Say this: "Liveness decides restarts, readiness decides traffic. Neither checks a shared dependency, because when it blips every copy fails together and a partial outage becomes a full one."

5 · Raft: a write commits when a majority stored it

Say this: "Raft gives one leader per term, chosen by a majority vote, and commits a write once a majority stored it. Two majorities always overlap, so a cut off old leader can never commit. Use 3 or 5 nodes."

6 · Fencing token: the storage rejects the paused client

Say this: "A lock with a timer cannot stop a client that froze and woke up late. The storage must check an increasing token. A plain Redis lock is fine for avoiding duplicate work, not for correctness."

7 · Worked example: the payment provider is slow

Say this: "Save the order as pending first. Then a 3 second timeout, one jittered retry with the order id as key, a bulkhead for payment calls, and a breaker that sends checkouts to a queue. The user gets an honest message; webhooks and reconciliation decide the end state."
